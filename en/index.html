Int. garage · day

The failure that wrote this manual

Our first test: a short series of shots of an old mechanic washing his Jaguar. On screen, it looked fine. Then we audited it, frame by frame.

  • From one shot to the next, the man switched sides of the car.
  • By the third shot, the end of the clip looked like a different man.
  • The sound jumped by 5 dB between shots.
  • About $17 of generation, nearly half of it on attempts we threw away.

One cause: we checked after the most expensive step. Since then, the studio runs on three laws.

  1. The face is non-negotiable.Any shot where the identity drifts is rejected. No exceptions.
  2. One shot, one action.One clean gesture at real speed. Never a stretched-out dance.
  3. Check before you pay.The image before the video, the draft before the final.

Then came “The Appointment”, our first short film: 12 shots, one man, zero cameras. Chapter 17 takes it apart shot by shot, scores included.

We measure. Even when it proves us wrong.

Questions

Before you decide

I'm a complete beginner. Is this for me?

Yes, if you're willing to go step by step. You don't need a powerful computer: generation runs in the cloud (only the optional checks run on your computer, about 2 GB). Count one or two evenings for a first shot checked by eye, one to two weeks for a first film. Want to test the waters first? That's what the free guide is for.

Which tools do I need, and what do they cost?

An image generator (Nano Banana, GPT Image, Seedream…), a video generator (Seedance, Veo, Kling…), ideally a reference-based platform such as Higgsfield, and a free editor (CapCut, DaVinci Resolve). They're not included. Prices we logged in November 2026: ≈ $0.07 per draft image, ≈ $0.27 per 2K image, ≈ $0.30 per 4 s of fast video, ≈ $1.60 per 4 s of video with sound. Your first exercise: about $3 to $8; a 6-shot film of about 30 s: about $35 to $50 including failed attempts, subscription not included (estimates). Prices move fast: double-check them.

Do I need to code?

No. The kit's scripts are a bonus: you can make your whole first film without them, using your video editor. To use them, you paste one command into a terminal; the one-time setup (Python environment included) is laid out step by step in chapter 2.

I'm on Windows. Will it work?

Yes. The Python scripts run directly (type python or py instead of python3). The two .sh scripts need WSL, with ffmpeg installed inside WSL; otherwise your video editor does the same job. On a Mac: Terminal and Homebrew.
